GMP QUALITY SYSTEM GUIDE — ICH Q10 / ICH Q8 / OOS / CAPA / CHANGE CONTROL
==========================================================================

SECTION 1: ICH Q10 PHARMACEUTICAL QUALITY SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------
ICH Q10 describes a model for an effective pharmaceutical quality system (PQS)
based on ISO Quality concepts, GMP regulations, and ICH guidelines Q8 and Q9.

The four main elements of ICH Q10 PQS are:
1. Process Performance and Product Quality Monitoring
2. Corrective Action and Preventive Action (CAPA) System
3. Change Management System
4. Management Review

ICH Q10 applies throughout the product lifecycle: pharmaceutical development,
technology transfer, commercial manufacturing, and product discontinuation.

Senior management is responsible for establishing, implementing, and maintaining
the PQS to achieve pharmaceutical quality objectives. Management must define
organizational structure, resource requirements, and performance indicators.

A well-designed PQS promotes continuous improvement, enabling the organization
to identify opportunities for product and process improvement, reduce variation,
and increase robustness.

SECTION 2: CAPA SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS
-------------------------------------
CAPA (Corrective Action and Preventive Action) is a fundamental element of GMP.
Under 21 CFR 211 and EU GMP Part I, CAPA processes must be established and maintained.

Corrective Action: Action taken to eliminate the cause of a detected nonconformity
or other undesirable situation to prevent recurrence.

Preventive Action: Action taken to eliminate the cause of a potential nonconformity
or other undesirable potential situation to prevent occurrence.

CAPA sources include: deviations, OOS results, complaints, audit findings,
trend analysis results, management review outputs, near-miss events.

Required CAPA documentation:
- Clear description of the problem or potential problem
- Root cause analysis methodology and results
- Description of corrective and/or preventive actions taken
- Verification/validation that the action does not adversely affect product
- Effectiveness check criteria and timeline
- Closure criteria and approval signatures
- Reference to regulatory basis where applicable

CAPA closure must include evidence of implementation and effectiveness verification.
Open CAPAs beyond 90 days require management escalation and justification.
Recurring CAPAs for the same root cause indicate systemic failures requiring
additional investigation and possible process redesign.

SECTION 3: DEVIATION CLASSIFICATION
-------------------------------------
Deviations from approved procedures or specifications must be classified by risk:

CRITICAL DEVIATION:
- Actual or potential impact on product safety, identity, strength, purity, or quality
- Affects a validated state of a critical process parameter (CPP)
- Breach of primary containment or sterility
- Potential risk to patient safety
- Requires immediate CAPA and QA notification within 24 hours
- Examples: sterility failure, mix-up of materials, unauthorized process change

MAJOR DEVIATION:
- Potential impact on product quality but not immediately life-threatening
- Departure from GMP requirements not classified as critical
- SOPs not followed for critical operations
- Documentation errors affecting batch traceability
- Requires CAPA within 30 days and thorough root cause investigation
- Examples: environmental monitoring exceedance, equipment cleaning failure

MINOR DEVIATION:
- No direct or indirect impact on product quality
- Low-risk procedural non-compliance
- Administrative documentation errors
- Requires corrective action but lower urgency
- Examples: late signature on logbook, minor scheduling deviation

All deviations regardless of classification must be documented, investigated,
and tracked in the deviation management system.

SECTION 4: TRENDING ANALYSIS
------------------------------
ICH Q10 requires systematic monitoring and trending of quality data to detect
signals and opportunities for improvement.

Key quality metrics to trend:
- Batch rejection and rework rates
- OOS and OOT (Out of Trend) results per product and per analytical method
- CAPA open/close rates and overdue CAPAs
- Deviation frequency by classification, product, process step, and equipment
- Complaint rates (number per batch, number per 1000 units distributed)
- Change control volume and cycle times
- Environmental monitoring exceedances by room and organism type
- Equipment calibration and PM overdue rates

Statistical methods for trending: control charts (X-bar, R, CUSUM), Pareto analysis,
run rules (Western Electric, Nelson), regression analysis for continuous data.

Trend reviews must occur at minimum annually as part of Annual Product Review (APR)
or Product Quality Review (PQR) per 21 CFR 211.180(e) and EU GMP Chapter 1.

Emerging trends must trigger investigation before specification exceedances occur.
OOT limits should be set tighter than OOS limits to provide early warning.

SECTION 5: SOP COMPLIANCE AND MANAGEMENT
------------------------------------------
Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) are controlled documents that describe
GMP activities in sufficient detail to ensure consistent execution.

SOP requirements per 21 CFR 211.100:
- Written procedures required for all production and process control activities
- Procedures must be followed and deviations documented
- SOPs must be reviewed and approved by QA before implementation
- Periodic review required (typically annually or based on risk)

SOP management system requirements:
- Unique document number and version control
- Effective date and superseded document tracking
- Distribution control and training requirements
- Change control process for revisions
- Archive of superseded versions with retention period

Training to SOPs must be documented, verified for comprehension,
and re-training required after revisions.

SECTION 6: MANAGEMENT REVIEW
------------------------------
ICH Q10 Section 3.4 requires periodic management review of the PQS.
Management review inputs must include:
- Quality policy and objectives achievement
- CAPA status (open, overdue, effectiveness)
- Deviation and complaint trends
- Audit results (internal, external, regulatory)
- Change control status
- Supplier qualification status
- Resource adequacy assessment
- PQR/APR summaries and conclusions

Management review outputs must include:
- Decisions and actions related to improvement of the PQS
- Resource needs identified
- Product and process improvement opportunities
- Risk mitigation actions

SECTION 7: ICH Q8 DESIGN SPACE AND QUALITY BY DESIGN
------------------------------------------------------
ICH Q8 introduces Quality by Design (QbD) concepts for pharmaceutical development.

Design Space: The multidimensional combination and interaction of input variables
(material attributes) and process parameters demonstrated to provide assurance of quality.

Working within the design space is not considered a change and does not require
regulatory notification. Movement out of design space is considered a change
and requires regulatory post-approval change management.

Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP): A prospective summary of quality characteristics
of a drug product that ideally will be achieved to ensure the desired quality,
safety, and efficacy.

Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs): Physical, chemical, biological, or microbiological
properties or characteristics that must be within an appropriate limit, range,
or distribution to ensure product quality.

Critical Process Parameters (CPPs): Process parameters whose variability has an
impact on a CQA and must be monitored or controlled to ensure the process
produces the desired quality.

Control Strategy: A planned set of controls derived from current product and process
understanding that assures process performance and product quality.

SECTION 8: OOS INVESTIGATION PROCEDURE
----------------------------------------
Out-of-Specification (OOS) results must be investigated per FDA Guidance on
Investigating OOS Test Results (2006) and 21 CFR 211.192.

Phase 1 — Laboratory Investigation:
- Conducted by analyst and laboratory supervisor
- Review calculation errors, instrument calibration, reagent lot, standard preparation
- Check sample preparation technique and sample integrity
- If assignable cause found: invalidate result with full documentation
- If no assignable cause: proceed to Phase 2

Phase 2 — Full Investigation:
- Extend investigation to manufacturing process
- Review batch record for process deviations
- Additional testing of retain and reserve samples
- Statistical evaluation (if multiple results available)
- Review of related batches

OOS investigation timeline: Phase 1 within 3 business days,
full investigation within 30 calendar days of OOS result.

OOS results cannot be averaged with passing results to achieve conformance.
Retesting is permissible only if an assignable cause is identified or for
statistical justification per predefined protocol.

SECTION 9: CHANGE CONTROL QA REVIEW
--------------------------------------
All changes to facilities, equipment, utilities, manufacturing processes, materials,
specifications, and computer systems must pass through change control per
21 CFR 211.100, EU GMP Chapter 1, and ICH Q10 Section 3.3.

QA review responsibilities in change control:
- Assess impact on product quality, safety, and regulatory compliance
- Determine validation/qualification requirements
- Identify required documentation updates (specifications, SOPs, batch records)
- Assess need for regulatory notification or filing
- Approve implementation timeline and risk mitigation measures
- Verify completion of all pre-implementation requirements before release

Change categories (based on risk assessment):
- Major Change: Requires prior regulatory approval before implementation
- Moderate Change: Requires regulatory notification (CBE-30 or Variation Type II)
- Minor Change: Annual reportable or no notification required

Post-implementation verification must confirm the change achieved intended
result without adverse impact. Change control records must be retained per
applicable retention schedule.

SECTION 7: OOS INVESTIGATION PROCEDURE
---------------------------------------
Out-of-Specification (OOS) investigation is required per 21 CFR 211.192.
Phase 1 - Laboratory Investigation (must be completed within 30 days):
Step 1: Review analyst's notebook and raw data for transcription errors
Step 2: Check instrument calibration status and last calibration date
Step 3: Verify reagent and standard preparation records
Step 4: Check system suitability results for chromatographic methods
Step 5: Perform Phase 1 calculations review
If assignable laboratory cause is found, document and retesting may proceed.
If no assignable cause: proceed to Phase 2 (full-scale investigation)

Phase 2 - Full-Scale OOS Investigation:
- Manufacturing process review
- Review of batch record for errors or deviations
- Retained sample testing by second analyst
- Statistical analysis if required
- If OOS is confirmed: initiate CAPA, reject batch, notify QA management

SECTION 8: ANNUAL PRODUCT REVIEW (APR/PQR)
--------------------------------------------
ICH Q10 requires Annual Product Review (APR) or Product Quality Review (PQR).
APR must include analysis of:
- All batches manufactured (% meeting specifications)
- OOS results and investigations
- Deviations and their CAPAs
- Stability data review
- Change control actions implemented
- Complaints and adverse events
- Returned goods analysis
- Qualification status of equipment and utilities
ICH Q10 4.2: APR should identify trends and determine product and process improvements.
APR must be signed by QA director and reviewed by management.

SECTION: DEVIATION MANAGEMENT AND CLASSIFICATION
A deviation is any departure from an approved procedure, specification, or standard.
Classification follows ICH Q10 and must be assigned within 24 hours of discovery.
All deviations must be documented, investigated, and closed with a root cause analysis.

DEVIATION CLASS: CRITICAL
Potential direct impact on patient safety, product identity, strength, purity, or quality.
Examples: sterility failure, identity error, out-of-specification potency greater than 10%,
incorrect label applied to container, temperature excursion above defined limits for biologics.
Required actions: Immediate QA notification within 1 hour, batch quarantine pending investigation,
expedited investigation report within 72 hours, regulatory notification if applicable,
mandatory CAPA with defined owner and target date.

DEVIATION CLASS: MAJOR
Significant GMP violation without immediate direct patient safety impact.
Examples: equipment calibration overdue more than 30 days, incomplete or missing batch record entry,
environmental monitoring exceedance in classified area, SOP deviation without documented impact assessment,
missing second-person verification for critical calculations.
Required actions: QA notification within 24 hours, formal investigation within 5 business days,
CAPA required if deviation is recurring or systemic, management review at next scheduled meeting.

DEVIATION CLASS: MINOR
Minor procedural deviation with no direct quality impact, confirmed by impact assessment.
Examples: documentation correction using approved method (single strikethrough, initials, date),
minor labeling error corrected before labeling operation complete, procedural step performed
out of sequence with no quality impact, minor delay within defined process time window.
Required actions: Documented in deviation log with brief impact statement, trending review quarterly,
CAPA at QA discretion based on frequency and risk.

SECTION: QUALITY MANAGEMENT REVIEW
Management review per ICH Q10 Section 3.4 must occur at defined intervals, minimum annually.
The review must be documented with minutes, action items, owners, and due dates.

Management review inputs must include all of the following:
Summary of all deviations with CAPA status and open items requiring escalation.
OOS results and investigations summary including any unresolved or repeated OOS events.
Audit and inspection findings from internal audits, customer audits, and regulatory inspections.
Product complaints, adverse events, and product recall or withdrawal history.

Additional management review inputs required by ICH Q10:
Change control summary including pending and recently implemented changes.
Process performance data and CPV (Continued Process Verification) trending results.
Equipment and utility qualification and calibration status overview.
Personnel training compliance rates by department and role.
Supplier qualification status including any new, changed, or disqualified suppliers.

Management review outputs must be documented and tracked:
Resource allocation decisions for quality infrastructure and staffing.
Process improvement actions with assigned owners and completion timelines.
Quality objectives for the next review period with measurable KPIs.
Effectiveness assessment of all actions from previous management review.
Any escalation decisions for unresolved quality issues.

SECTION: SOP CONTROL AND COMPLIANCE
Standard Operating Procedures must be controlled documents per 21 CFR 211.68 and EU GMP Chapter 4.
Every SOP must have a unique identifier, version number, effective date, and authorized signatures.

SOP lifecycle requirements:
Review cycle must not exceed 2 years between formal periodic reviews.
Revision triggers include: process changes, equipment changes, regulatory updates, deviation findings.
All revisions must go through the same approval workflow as original document.
Superseded versions must be archived and prevented from use at the point of care.

SOP approval and training requirements:
Quality Assurance must approve all SOPs before the effective date.
All personnel performing tasks described in the SOP must be trained before the effective date.
Training must be documented: trainer name, trainee name, date, SOP version, assessment method.
Competency must be verified — reading acknowledgment alone is insufficient for GMP activities.

SOP compliance monitoring:
Periodic effectiveness review must confirm SOPs are being followed in practice.
Self-inspection program must include random SOP compliance checks during operations.
Any deviation from an approved SOP requires a deviation report, regardless of quality impact.
SOPs found to be consistently not followed must be evaluated for revision or additional training.

SECTION: CHANGE CONTROL MANAGEMENT
Change control is a formal system to evaluate, approve, and document changes to GMP systems.
Per 21 CFR 211.68 and ICH Q10, all changes that could affect product quality must be controlled.

Change categories and requirements:
Major change: Affects product specifications, validated processes, or regulatory filings.
Requires: Risk assessment, validation/qualification plan, QA and regulatory approval before implementation.
Minor change: Non-critical changes with documented low risk to product quality.
Requires: Impact assessment by process owner, QA review and approval, no validation required unless risk-based.
Emergency change: Immediate action required to prevent safety risk or production stoppage.
Requires: Retrospective documentation within 24 hours and full change control within 5 business days.

Change control documentation requirements:
Change Request form with: description, reason, risk assessment, affected systems and documents.
Pre-approval by Quality Assurance before any implementation begins.
Implementation record: who performed the change, date and time, verification steps completed.
Post-implementation review: confirmation that the change achieved its intent with no unexpected effects.
Update of all affected SOPs, validation documents, and training materials.

SECTION: SUPPLIER QUALIFICATION AND MANAGEMENT
All suppliers of APIs, excipients, packaging materials, and critical services must be qualified.
Per 21 CFR 211.84 and EU GMP Chapter 5, materials must not be used without supplier approval.

Supplier qualification process:
Initial qualification: supplier questionnaire, quality agreement, audit or audit waiver with justification.
Approved Supplier List (ASL): maintained by QA, reviewed at minimum annually.
Qualification categories: Approved (full audit), Conditionally Approved (audit pending), Disqualified.

Ongoing supplier management requirements:
Annual supplier performance review based on: delivery performance, complaint history, COA compliance.
Re-qualification audit required if: product quality complaint confirmed, major change at supplier facility,
FDA warning letter or import alert issued to supplier, lapse in supply greater than 2 years.
Critical material suppliers (API, primary packaging) must have a signed Quality Technical Agreement.
Quality agreements must address: specifications, change notification requirements, audit rights, GMP compliance.

SECTION: OUT-OF-SPECIFICATION INVESTIGATIONS
OOS results must be investigated per FDA OOS Guidance (2006) using a two-phase approach.
All OOS results must be documented immediately and batch must be placed on hold.

Phase 1: Laboratory Investigation (must be completed before any retest)
Review all analyst records, calculations, and instrument logs for the failing test.
Confirm sample preparation, instrument calibration, and standard performance were acceptable.
Check for assignable laboratory errors: pipetting errors, incorrect standard, transcription errors.
Document findings with specificity — general statements like "no errors found" are not acceptable.
Phase 1 must be completed within 5 business days. If assignable error found: invalidate result, retest.

Phase 2: Full-Scale OOS Investigation (when no laboratory error found in Phase 1)
Expand investigation to manufacturing: review batch record, process parameters, equipment logs.
Additional testing: retain samples from multiple time points, test additional units from same batch.
Statistical analysis: apply appropriate statistical tools to distinguish process variability from failure.
Root cause conclusion must be definitive — inconclusive is not acceptable for batch disposition.
Batch must not be released until OOS investigation is formally closed with documented disposition decision.

SECTION: ENVIRONMENTAL MONITORING IN GMP FACILITIES
Environmental monitoring programs must be designed, validated, and executed per EU GMP Annex 1 and USP <1116>.
All classified areas used for sterile or aseptic manufacturing require ongoing environmental controls.

Classification and monitoring requirements for cleanrooms:
Grade A (ISO 5): Highest risk zones — filling area, stopper bowl, open product containers, aseptic connections.
Requires: Continuous particle monitoring, viable air sampling every session, surface sampling every session.
Grade B (ISO 5 at rest, ISO 7 in operation): Background environment for Grade A zone.
Requires: Particle monitoring at defined intervals, viable air and surface sampling per approved schedule.
Grade C (ISO 8 in operation): Less critical manufacturing steps such as solution preparation for filtration.
Grade D: Background area for handling of less-critical components.

Action and alert limits for environmental monitoring:
Alert limit: Trend indicator — result above this value triggers investigation but not batch rejection.
Action limit: Regulatory limit — result above this value requires immediate CAPA and impact assessment on batch.
Limits must be established based on historical data and approved in the monitoring program procedure.
Exceeding action limit requires: batch impact assessment, investigation report, QA notification, and CAPA.

Environmental monitoring trending requirements:
Monthly review of all environmental monitoring data for trends toward action limits.
Contamination events must be investigated for root cause regardless of whether action limit was exceeded.
Annual review of alert and action limits to confirm they remain appropriate for the current process.

SECTION: COMPLAINTS AND ADVERSE EVENT MANAGEMENT
Customer complaints and adverse events must be managed per 21 CFR 211.198 and EU GMP Chapter 8.
Every complaint must be evaluated for potential regulatory reporting obligation.

Complaint intake and triage requirements:
All complaints must be documented regardless of the communication channel: written, verbal, or electronic.
Initial assessment within 24 hours to classify: product quality complaint, adverse event, or general feedback.
Adverse events involving serious injury or death require expedited review within 24 hours by Medical Affairs and QA.
Retain complaint sample if available — do not return to complainant until investigation is complete.

Complaint investigation requirements:
Investigation must verify: batch number, expiration date, manufacturing history, and distribution records.
Laboratory analysis of retain samples required when complaint alleges quality defect.
Root cause conclusion must address: was the complaint confirmed, what was the cause, was it isolated or systemic.
Trending analysis required: same product, same batch, same defect type reported by multiple customers is a signal.

Regulatory reporting thresholds:
FDA MedWatch: report when the product may have caused or contributed to serious injury or death.
Field Alert Report (FAR): within 3 business days for confirmed contamination, significant chemical or physical change.
EU Rapid Alert: within 24 hours for serious quality defects that may require recall action.
Failure to report within required timeframes is a GMP violation independent of the underlying complaint.
