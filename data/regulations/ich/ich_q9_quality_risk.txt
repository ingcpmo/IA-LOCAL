TITLE: ICH Q9 Quality Risk Management
SOURCE: International Council for Harmonisation

DEFINITION: Quality Risk Management is a systematic process for the assessment,
control, communication and review of risks to the quality of the drug product
across the product lifecycle.

FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis):
A prospective tool used to identify and rank failure modes according to their
severity, occurrence probability, and detectability.
- RPN (Risk Priority Number) = Severity x Occurrence x Detectability
- RPN scale typically 1-10 for each factor
- High RPN (>100-125): requires immediate corrective action

RISK ASSESSMENT PRINCIPLES:
1. Identify hazards (potential failure modes)
2. Analyze risk (probability and severity of harm)
3. Evaluate risk (compare against criteria)
4. Control risk (reduce to acceptable level)
5. Review risk (ongoing monitoring)

QUALIFICATION RISK ASSESSMENT:
Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs): physical, chemical, biological attributes
that must be within appropriate limits to ensure product quality.
Critical Process Parameters (CPPs): parameters that affect CQAs and must be
monitored and controlled.

GAMP 5 RISK-BASED APPROACH:
Category 1: Infrastructure software (OS, databases)
Category 3: Non-configured software (instruments)
Category 4: Configured software (SCADA, DCS, LIMS)
Category 5: Custom software (bespoke applications)
Higher category = higher risk = more validation effort required.
